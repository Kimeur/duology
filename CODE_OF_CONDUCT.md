# Code of conduct

## Our promise

Duology is an open project, and we want everyone to be able to take part: people new to iOS and people who have shipped for years, people who don't write English as a first language, and people of every background, identity and ability. We, the people who maintain Duology, promise to keep the project a place where that works.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient. Assume good intent, and ask before you assume.
- Critique code, designs and claims, not the people who made them.
- Back technical claims with a source, and accept a correction that comes with one. Being wrong about an API is normal here; that's why we ask for sources.
- Credit other people's work. Don't present copied text or code as your own.
- Respect "no", whether it's about a feature, a review comment or a private message.
- Help newcomers find their way, and say so when you're new yourself.

## What we don't accept

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or jokes at someone's expense.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwanted attention of any kind.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address or a private message, without their permission.
- Trolling, deliberate derailing, or repeated off-topic promotion.
- Pressuring someone to keep talking to you after they've asked you to stop.

## Where this applies

In issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and commit messages, and anywhere you represent Duology in public, such as a talk or a social media account that speaks for the project.

## How to report

If something here makes you feel unsafe or unwelcome, tell the maintainer, [@Kimeur](https://github.com/Kimeur), privately:

1. Use the private form at [Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/Kimeur/duology/security/advisories/new) and start the title with "Conduct:". The report isn't public.
2. If that form isn't available, open an issue titled "Conduct contact request" with no details. The maintainer will reply with a private way to talk.

For content that breaks GitHub's own rules, you can also [report it to GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

We keep the reporter's identity private and won't share details without permission.

## What happens next

The maintainer reviews every report and responds in proportion to the harm:

1. **A private note** explaining what went wrong, for a first, minor problem.
2. **A warning**, with clear limits on future behaviour.
3. **A temporary ban** from the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for repeated or serious harm.

These rules apply to the maintainer too. If your report is about the maintainer, you can [report it to GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam) instead.
